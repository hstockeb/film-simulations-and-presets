## Install

[Download a zip](https://github.com/hstockeb/film-simulations-and-presets/releases/latest) — everything, one half, or one family — and import it
as it is, no need to unzip:

- **Lightroom Classic:** Develop → Presets → right-click → *Import Presets*
- **Lightroom:** Presets → ⋮ → *Import Presets*

They sort themselves into folders in the Presets panel.

## Using them

1. **Set your white balance first.** The presets never touch it.
2. **Film simulations: expose first, then apply.** Like film, each has its own
   fixed contrast, so get the exposure right and let the film do the rest.
3. **Presets adapt to the photo on their own.** The few marked *(no auto)* have
   a deliberately faded or dark base — set the exposure by hand on those.
4. **Dial the strength** with the *Amount* slider above the presets: around 75
   softens a look for portraits, 150 pushes a gentle one harder.

Too warm or too cool? Lower the look's Amount rather than moving Temperature.
On some cameras you may want to nudge a slider a few points — no more.

Sharpening and noise reduction are left to you, so your own settings stay as
they are.

## About the looks

Each simulation was researched against film datasheets, labs and photographers'
notes, then built from scratch. They're interpretations: no manufacturer
profiles are copied, and film and camera names only say what a look is reaching
for. The two in *Personal* are my own, made for single trips on one camera.

All samples are the same frame from Lake Villarrica, Chile, developed at the
same exposure — a look that seems darker here really is darker.
